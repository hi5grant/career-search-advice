# Evaluating a Company and a Role

Do not evaluate a job posting in isolation. A compelling title at a fragile company—or a stable company with a poorly matched role—may not be a good move.

Organize the work at two levels: **company** and **opportunity**. Company research is reusable; each distinct job posting gets its own evaluation and application record.

## Company research

Create one folder per company under `companies/`, using its recognizable Title Case name with spaces. Keep company-wide research in that folder so it can inform multiple applications.

Investigate:

- **Business model:** who pays, for what, and how recurring the revenue is.
- **Revenue and growth:** reported figures, credible estimates, and trends.
- **Profitability and cash:** operating income, free cash flow, burn rate, cash runway, and funding needs where available.
- **Ownership and financing:** public/private status, investors, debt, funding history, acquisitions.
- **Business durability:** customer concentration, competition, regulation, pricing power, and dependence on partners.
- **Strategy:** priorities, product direction, expansion, layoffs, restructuring, and leadership changes.
- **Engineering environment:** technical writing, architecture posts, reliability expectations, hiring patterns, and team structure.
- **Recruiting landscape:** relevant recruiters and talent leaders, with public evidence and links.

Save company-level findings in `company-research.md` and recruiter/contact research in `recruiters.md`. Label each finding as a company-reported fact, regulatory filing, reputable third-party estimate, or analysis. Private-company financial data is often unavailable; do not present estimates as audited facts.

## Role and application records

Every distinct requisition, posting, or talent-pipeline application gets its own folder under the company's `job-roles/` directory. Use a readable unique name such as `YYYY-MM - Role Title`. Do not reuse one folder for a different posting or overwrite a prior application's materials.

Keep these artifacts together when applicable:

- `job-posting.md`: exact title, requisition ID, public URL, date captured, location, compensation, summary, requirements, and ambiguities.
- `match.md`: role-specific evidence comparison, gaps, risks, recommendation, score, and confidence.
- `application-notes.md`: status, submission date, recruiter conversations, answers, follow-ups, and interview notes.
- `resume.md` or a role-specific document: final resume tailored to that exact opportunity.
- Optional cover letter and interview-preparation materials.

A general future-opportunities form is still one opportunity, but label it accurately. Do not treat a broad talent pipeline as a confirmed Staff/Principal requisition, hiring commitment, or compensation band.

## Compare the role with your inventory

Extract core responsibilities, expected outcomes, required versus preferred skills, scope, architecture authority, people-management expectations, technical requirements, on-call/travel/location expectations, and compensation where disclosed.

Classify evidence as:

- **Direct match:** supported by specific evidence.
- **Adjacent/transferable:** similar problem or technology, but not identical.
- **Gap:** no meaningful evidence.
- **Unknown:** needs clarification.

Do not call an adjacent skill a direct match. A gap is not automatically disqualifying; assess whether it is central to the role.

## Example scoring rubric

A 100-point rubric can help compare opportunities:

- Role and responsibility alignment: 30
- Technical/domain alignment: 25
- Company stability and business quality: 20
- Compensation and practical fit: 15
- Growth and career direction: 10

Change the weights to fit your priorities. A score is a decision aid, not objective truth. Explain the rationale and list deal-breakers separately. Keep company durability and role fit visible as distinct dimensions; a strong company does not automatically make a particular opening a good fit.

## Questions to resolve

- Is the role genuinely remote, and from which locations?
- Is the posting a real requisition or a general pipeline?
- Is the posted scope consistent with the likely level?
- Does the company have a credible business and funding model?
- Are compensation and benefits aligned with your requirements?
- What are the largest gaps, and can you explain them honestly?
- What would make you decline the offer even if received?
