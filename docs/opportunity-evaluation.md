# Evaluating a Company and a Role

Do not evaluate a job posting in isolation. A compelling title at a fragile company—or a stable company with a poorly matched role—may not be a good move.

## Company research

Investigate:

- **Business model:** who pays, for what, and how recurring the revenue is.
- **Revenue and growth:** reported figures, credible estimates, and trends.
- **Profitability and cash:** operating income, free cash flow, burn rate, cash runway, and funding needs where available.
- **Ownership and financing:** public/private status, investors, debt, funding history, acquisitions.
- **Business durability:** customer concentration, competition, regulation, pricing power, and dependence on partners.
- **Strategy:** priorities, product direction, expansion, layoffs, restructuring, and leadership changes.
- **Engineering environment:** technical writing, architecture posts, reliability expectations, hiring patterns, and team structure.

Label each finding as a company-reported fact, regulatory filing, reputable third-party estimate, or analysis. Private-company financial data is often unavailable; do not present estimates as audited facts.

## Role research

Extract core responsibilities, expected outcomes, required versus preferred skills, scope, architecture authority, people-management expectations, technical requirements, on-call/travel/location expectations, and compensation where disclosed.

## Compare the role with your inventory

- **Direct match:** supported by specific evidence.
- **Adjacent/transferable:** similar problem or technology, but not identical.
- **Gap:** no meaningful evidence.
- **Unknown:** needs clarification.

Do not call an adjacent skill a direct match. A gap is not automatically disqualifying; assess whether it is central to the role.

## Example scoring rubric

A 100-point rubric can help compare opportunities:

- Role and responsibility alignment: 30
- Technical/domain alignment: 25
- Company stability and business quality: 20
- Compensation and practical fit: 15
- Growth and career direction: 10

Change the weights to fit your priorities. A score is a decision aid, not objective truth. Explain the rationale and list deal-breakers separately.

## Questions to resolve

- Is the role genuinely remote, and from which locations?
- Is the posted scope consistent with the likely level?
- Does the company have a credible business and funding model?
- Are compensation and benefits aligned with your requirements?
- What are the largest gaps, and can you explain them honestly?
- What would make you decline the offer even if received?
