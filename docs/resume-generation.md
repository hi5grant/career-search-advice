# Generating a Targeted Resume

A targeted resume should be selective, not fictional. The master inventory remains unchanged; the resume selects evidence that best supports the role.

## Workflow

1. Open the specific opportunity folder under `companies/<Company Name>/job-roles/`.
2. Save the job description and source URL in that folder's `job-posting.md`.
3. Extract responsibilities, required skills, and seniority signals.
4. Compare each requirement with the inventory.
5. Identify direct matches, transferable evidence, gaps, and unresolved claims.
6. Ask the candidate about material uncertainties before drafting.
7. Select relevant projects and outcomes. Use a company-level base resume as a starting point only when it is relevant.
8. Draft a concise, ATS-readable resume and save it inside the exact opportunity folder.
9. Review every claim for accuracy and interview defensibility.

Do not save one generic "latest resume" over the top of previous applications. Each role needs a distinct, traceable version. Company base resumes are reusable starting points; role-specific resumes are the final application artifacts.

## Writing rules

- Use standard headings and a simple layout.
- Prefer clear text over columns, icons, graphics, or skill bars.
- Use keywords naturally when supported by experience.
- Lead bullets with specific actions and explain the result or scope.
- Avoid vague claims such as "visionary leader" or "expert in everything."
- Do not inflate titles, authority, team size, or architecture ownership.
- Do not imply formal people management if the role was technical leadership.
- Keep personal projects separate from employment.
- Do not include internal notes, VERIFY markers, or speculative claims.

## Example transformation

Inventory evidence:

> Coordinated multiple teams delivering cloud-native components. Helped resolve architecture tradeoffs between serverless APIs and container workloads. Another designated architect retained final approval.

Possible resume bullet:

> Guided cross-team architecture tradeoffs for a cloud-native modernization, evaluating serverless and container-based API patterns with implementation and operational constraints in view.

This is stronger than a generic claim and does not falsely claim sole architecture authority.

## Final review

For every bullet, ask:
- Can I explain exactly what I did?
- Can I distinguish my contribution from the team's?
- Can I explain the technology at the depth implied?
- Is the metric measured and attributable?
- Is the project safe to disclose publicly?
- Would I be comfortable being challenged on this in an interview?

If any answer is no, revise or remove the claim.
